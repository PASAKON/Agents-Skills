# MoonieX Content Skill

One skill for everything the MoonieX org writes: persuasive copy, edits, content plans, a
reusable brand voice, platform-native posts, and long-form articles. It replaces several
overlapping content skills.

**How to use this file:** jump to the section that matches the request. Sections are
self-contained. When voice consistency matters across more than one deliverable, run
**§5 Brand Voice** first and reuse its `VOICE PROFILE` everywhere else.

Provenance for each section is tagged `(best-of: <source>)`. See `SOURCES.md` for the
full attribution + license table and `NOTICE.md` for third-party notices.

## 1. Copywriting (best-of: copywriting)

Expert direct-response copy that persuades and converts.

### 1.1 Gather requirements first
Ask if not provided: **page type** · **target audience** (pains, psychographics) ·
**primary goal/action** · **product/service** · **key differentiator** · **tone** ·
**constraints** (word count, brand rules, required elements).

### 1.2 Pick a framework (default: PAS)
- **PAS — Problem · Agitation · Solution.** Pain-driven products, B2B SaaS, health, finance.
- **AIDA — Attention · Interest · Desire · Action.** Cold audiences, ads, top-of-funnel.
- **BAB — Before · After · Bridge.** Transformation stories, coaching, onboarding.
- **4Ps — Promise · Picture · Proof · Push.** Sales pages, high-ticket, webinars.

PAS example:
```
Problem:  "You spend 3 hours every week manually updating spreadsheets."
Agitate:  "That's 156 hours a year — nearly a full month — lost to data entry
           a machine could do in seconds."
Solution: "Acme Sync updates your spreadsheets in real time. Set it once.
           Never copy-paste again."
```

### 1.3 Headlines — write 5–10, then rank
| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| How to [outcome] without [pain] | How to Double Your Traffic Without Paying for Ads |
| The Secret to [outcome] | The Secret to Headlines That Convert at 30% |
| [N] Ways to [outcome] | 7 Ways to Reduce Churn This Quarter |
| Why [common belief] Is Wrong | Why "More Content" Is the Worst SEO Advice |
| [Do this] Before [consequence] | Fix Your Meta Descriptions Before Google Does |
| Stop [bad habit]. Start [good habit]. | Stop Guessing Keywords. Start Using Data. |
| The [adjective] Guide to [topic] | The No-BS Guide to Email Deliverability |
| [N] [topic] Mistakes (And How to Fix Them) | 5 Landing Page Mistakes Costing You Conversions |
| I [did X] in [timeframe]. Here's How. | I Grew to 10K Subscribers in 90 Days. Here's How. |

**Headline checklist:** clear benefit · specific audience · curiosity/urgency ·
under ~70 chars for SEO · no claim the content can't deliver · one power word.

**Power words** (use 1–2, rotate): *Urgency* Now/Today/Instantly/Limited/Last chance ·
*Exclusivity* Secret/Insider/Exclusive/Members-only · *Trust* Proven/Guaranteed/Verified/
Research-backed · *Value* Free/Bonus/Save/Effortless/Unlock · *Emotion* Remarkable/
Breakthrough/Powerful/Life-changing.

### 1.4 CTAs
Rules: start with a verb · name the outcome ("Start My Free Trial" > "Submit") ·
reduce friction ("No credit card required") · match commitment to funnel stage ·
test first-person ("Start My…" often > "Start Your…").

| Funnel stage | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Submit | Get the Free Guide |
| Consideration | Learn More | See How [Product] Works |
| Decision | Buy | Start My 14-Day Free Trial |
| Retention | Continue | Unlock Premium Features |

Placement: primary CTA above the fold; secondary after each argument-building section;
final CTA at the bottom with a value recap; consider a sticky CTA on long pages.

### 1.5 Tone presets
- **Professional** — full sentences, data-led, minimal slang. B2B, finance, healthcare.
- **Casual** — contractions, short sentences, "you," analogies. DTC, consumer apps.
- **Urgent** — fragments OK, time-bound ("Ends midnight," "3 spots left"), bold key phrases. Launches, flash deals.
- **Authoritative** — declaratives, no hedging, cite results. Thought leadership, premium brands.

### 1.6 Landing-page section order
Hero (headline + subhead + CTA) → social-proof bar → problem → solution →
features/benefits (3–6, benefit-led headlines) → how it works (3 steps) →
testimonials (2–3, results-specific) → pricing (highlight one plan) →
FAQ (5–8 objection-handlers) → final CTA.

### 1.7 Deliver
5–10 ranked headlines · full copy by section · 3–5 CTA variants with reasoning ·
tone check vs brief · estimated Flesch-Kincaid grade · 2–3 unprompted conversion ideas.

### 1.8 Flag these mistakes
Features without benefits · company-centric ("we/our" > "you/your") · weak generic CTAs ·
no social proof · CTA buried with no early CTA · audience-wrong jargon · claims without proof.

## 2. Copy Editing (best-of: copy-editing)

Take existing copy and make every sentence sharper, clearer, more persuasive.

### 2.1 Process
1. **Read the whole piece** — goal, audience, intended tone, primary CTA.
2. **Structural pass** — does the opener hook? logical problem→solution→CTA flow?
   reorder/merge/cut sections? CTA placed well (above fold + end)?
3. **Line-by-line** — apply the checklist; give before/after + reason per change.
4. **Final polish** — read aloud; check rhythm, tense/POV consistency; score readability.

### 2.2 Editing checklist (flag every violation)
- **Clarity** — every sentence understood on first read. Fix ambiguous pronouns, abstractions, buried leads. Concrete > abstract; lead with value.
- **Conciseness** — cut 20–30% on first drafts. Replace bloat:
  `in order to`→to · `due to the fact that`→because · `at this point in time`→now ·
  `has the ability to`→can · `make use of`→use · `it is important to note that`→(delete) ·
  `a large number of`→many · `prior to`→before · `each and every`→every.
  Run the **"So what?" test** on each sentence; if it doesn't advance the argument, cut it.
- **Active voice** — ≥80% active. Detect passives: form of *to be* + past participle (if "by zombies" fits after the verb, it's passive). "Your data is protected by our encryption" → "Our encryption protects your data."
- **Jargon** — replace unless the audience demonstrably uses it. "Leverage synergies" → "work together more effectively." *Exception:* technical readers expect precise terms (API, latency) — don't dumb those down.
- **Emotional impact** — specificity creates emotion. "Save time" → "Get home for dinner instead of staying late to fix reports." Show stakes ("Every day without this costs you $47 in wasted ad spend"). Use micro-stories.
- **Consistency** — capitalization style, formatting, tense, POV, terminology, exact brand name, Oxford comma, number formatting.
- **Grammar/mechanics** — subject-verb agreement, dangling modifiers, comma splices, run-ons, its/it's, your/you're, parallel lists, correct dashes, spelling (esp. product/technical names).

### 2.3 Hemingway principles
Short sentences (split >25 words) · simple words (use > utilize) · cut adverbs (the verb is too weak) · drop qualifiers (very/really/quite) · one thought per sentence · delete throat-clearing ("It goes without saying") · end sentences on a strong word.

### 2.4 Readability targets (Flesch-Kincaid)
| Grade | Audience | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | General public | Consumer ads, social, DTC pages |
| 6–8 | Educated general | Blogs, email, most landing pages |
| 8–10 | Professional | B2B, whitepapers, enterprise |
| 10–12 | Specialist | Technical/legal docs |
| 12+ | Expert | Usually too complex — simplify |

Most marketing copy should land at grade 6–8.

### 2.5 Deliver
Summary of changes · edited copy · change-log table (location · original · edited · reason) ·
before/after readability grade · remaining suggestions (structure, missing social proof/CTA/FAQ, data to add).

### 2.6 Always call out
No CTA · feature dump without benefits · wall of text · inconsistent tone ·
weasel words ("up to," "potentially") · clichés ("best-in-class," "game-changer") ·
self-centered copy (flip we:you ratio).

## 3. Content Strategy (best-of: content-strategy)

Comprehensive plans that drive organic traffic, nurture leads, and support business goals.

### 3.1 Gather requirements
Business type · target audience/ICPs · business goals · current state (assets, traffic,
top performers) · 3–5 competitors · resources (team, budget, cadence) · channels · timeline.

### 3.2 Topic clusters
A **pillar page** (2,000–5,000-word guide) + 8–20 **cluster pages** (800–1,500 words)
interlinking to the pillar and each other — signals topical authority.

Build: (1) identify 3–5 core topics → (2) define a pillar per topic (title, target keyword,
length, format) → (3) map 10–20 long-tail cluster articles per pillar → (4) linking rules:
every cluster → its pillar (required), pillar → every cluster (required), clusters → 2–3 siblings (recommended), descriptive anchors only.

Cluster template:
```markdown
## Cluster: [Topic]
### Pillar
- Title · Target keyword (vol/difficulty) · URL · Word count (3000–5000) · Format
### Cluster pages
| # | Title | Keyword | Vol | Difficulty | Words | Priority |
### Internal linking map
- Pillar → all clusters; Cluster 1 → Cluster 3, 5; …
```

### 3.3 Buyer-journey mapping
| Stage | Reader mindset | Content types | Intent | KPIs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness (ToFu)** | "I have a problem, don't know the fix." | what/why posts, how-to guides, reports, explainers | Informational | Traffic, time on page, signups |
| **Consideration (MoFu)** | "Which option is best?" | X-vs-Y, "best [cat] for [use]," case studies, webinars, templates | Commercial investigation | Downloads, demo requests |
| **Decision (BoFu)** | "Convince me to buy." | product/pricing pages, testimonials, trials, ROI calculators | Transactional | Trials, bookings, revenue |
| **Retention** | "How do I get the most from this?" | onboarding, best practices, update notes, advanced guides | — | Activation, adoption, NPS, churn |

### 3.4 Content mix
Awareness/educational 40% · consideration/comparison 25% · decision/conversion 15% ·
retention/enablement 10% · thought-leadership/brand 10%.

### 3.5 Cadence by stage
| Stage | Blog | Newsletter | Social | Video |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early (0–50K) | 2–4/wk | 1/wk | 5/wk | 1/mo |
| Growth (50–200K) | 3–5/wk | 1–2/wk | 7/wk | 2/mo |
| Scale (200K+) | 5–10/wk | 2–3/wk | 10+/wk | 4/mo |

Batching: wk1 research+outline the month · wk2 first drafts · wk3 edit+assets · wk4 publish+promote+repurpose.

### 3.6 Distribution
- **Owned** — blog (full), newsletter (summary + link), social (3–5 posts/article), podcast/video repurpose.
- **Earned** — SEO, backlink pitches (guest posts, roundups, resource pages), PR if newsworthy, communities (Slack/Discord/Reddit/forums).
- **Paid** — boost top organic, retarget non-converters, sponsor niche newsletters.

**Repurposing — 1 blog post → 8+:** newsletter summary · X/thread · LinkedIn long-form ·
IG carousel · 60-sec video script · infographic · podcast outline · 3–5 quote graphics.

### 3.7 Deliver
Content audit · topic-cluster map · buyer-journey map · 90-day calendar (title, keyword,
stage, type, owner, due) · distribution playbook · KPI dashboard with targets ·
detailed briefs for month 1 (title, intent, H2/H3 outline, competitor to beat, internal links, CTA).

## 5. Brand Voice (best-of: ECC brand-voice)

> **License note:** the workflow and `VOICE PROFILE` schema below derive from ECC's MIT-licensed
> `brand-voice` skill (© 2026 Affaan Mustafa). See `SOURCES.md` / `NOTICE.md`.

Build a durable, source-derived voice profile once, then reuse it across every other section
instead of re-deriving style or defaulting to generic AI copy.

### 5.1 When to run
The user wants content/outreach in a specific voice · writing for X/LinkedIn/email/launch ·
matching a known author's tone · any time >1 downstream output needs voice consistency.

### 5.2 Source priority (strongest real signal first)
1. recent original posts/threads (raw voice)
2. articles, essays, memos, launch notes, newsletters
3. real outbound emails/DMs that worked
4. product docs, changelogs, README framing, site copy (often edited — least authentic)

Gather 5–20 samples (or ≥500 words). Prefer recent unless older is explicitly canonical.
Don't use generic platform exemplars as source. If samples are thin, say so and offer a quick snapshot only.

### 5.3 Extract
Rhythm & sentence length · compression vs explanation · capitalization norms · parenthetical
use · question frequency & purpose · how sharply claims are made · how often numbers/
mechanisms/receipts appear · how transitions work · **what the author never does**.

### 5.4 Output — `VOICE PROFILE` block (reusable downstream)
```text
VOICE PROFILE
=============
Author:
Goal:
Confidence:

Source Set
- source 1 / 2 / 3

Rhythm           - sentence length, pacing, fragmentation
Compression      - dense vs explanatory
Capitalization   - conventional / mixed / situational
Parentheticals   - how used and not used
Question Use     - rare / frequent / rhetorical / absent
Claim Style      - how claims are framed, supported, sharpened
Preferred Moves  - concrete moves the author DOES use
Banned Moves     - specific patterns the author does NOT use (must be observable in source)
CTA Rules        - how/when/whether to close with asks
Channel Notes    - X: / LinkedIn: / Email:
```
Keep it short, concrete, source-backed — bullets not essays. If sources conflict, **call out
the split** instead of averaging into mush.

### 5.5 Hard bans (delete + rewrite anywhere they appear)
Fake curiosity hooks · "not X, just Y" · "no fluff" · forced lowercase · LinkedIn thought-leader
cadence · bait questions · "Excited to share" · generic founder-journey filler · corny parentheticals.

### 5.6 Persistence
Reuse the latest confirmed `VOICE PROFILE` across related tasks in the same session.
Don't write repo-tracked files storing a personal voice fingerprint unless the user explicitly asks.

## 6. Channel Adaptation (best-of: ECC content-engine)

Platform-native content without flattening the author's real voice into platform slop.

**Non-negotiables:** start from source material, not generic formulas · adapt the *format*
to the platform, not the persona · one post = one real claim · specificity > adjectives ·
no engagement bait unless asked.

**Per-platform rules:**
- **X** — open with the strongest claim/artifact/tension; keep compression; in a thread each post must advance the argument; no unneeded context.
- **LinkedIn** — expand just enough for outsiders to follow; no fake lesson post unless the source is genuinely reflective; no corporate-inspiration cadence; no "journey" filler.
- **Short video** — script around the visual sequence; first seconds show the result/problem/punch; don't write narration that reads better than it plays.
- **YouTube** — show the result/tension early; organize by argument, not filler; chapter only when it aids clarity.
- **Newsletter** — open with the point/conflict/artifact; no warm-up paragraph; every section adds something new.

**Repurposing flow:** pick anchor asset → extract 3–7 atomic claims/scenes → rank by
sharpness/novelty/proof → one strong idea per output → adapt structure per platform →
strip platform-shaped filler → run quality gate.

**Quality gate:** sounds like the author (not the platform stereotype) · real claim/proof
per draft · no hype language · no fake engagement bait · no duplicated copy across platforms
unless requested · any CTA is earned + user-approved.

## 7. Long-Form Writing (best-of: ECC article-writing)

Articles, guides, tutorials, essays, newsletters that sound like a person with a point of
view, not an LLM smoothed into paste.

**Core rules:** lead with the concrete thing (artifact, example, number, screenshot, code) ·
explain *after* the example · keep sentences tight unless the source voice is expansive ·
proof over adjectives · never invent facts, credibility, or customer evidence.

**Voice:** if a specific voice matters, run §5 first and reuse its `VOICE PROFILE` — don't
do a second style pass. No references → default to a sharp operator voice: concrete, unsentimental, useful.

**Process:** clarify audience + purpose → hard outline, one job per section → start sections
with proof/artifact/conflict/example → expand only where the next sentence earns space →
cut anything templated or self-congratulatory.

**Structure:** *Technical guides* — open with what the reader gets; concrete output in each
major section; end with actionable takeaways. *Essays* — open with tension/contradiction;
one argument thread per section; opinions answer to evidence. *Newsletters* — first screen
does real work; no diary filler; labels only if they aid scanability.

**Banned patterns:** "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" · "game-changer / cutting-edge /
revolutionary" · "here's why this matters" as a bare bridge · fake vulnerability arcs ·
a closing question added only to juice engagement · biography padding · AI throat-clearing.

**Quality gate:** claims backed by provided sources · generic AI transitions gone · voice
matches examples / agreed `VOICE PROFILE` · every section adds something new · formatting fits the medium.

## 9. Cross-Cutting Rules (apply to every section)

1. **Reader over brand.** More "you/your" than "we/our." Flip the ratio if inverted.
2. **Proof over adjectives.** Specific numbers, artifacts, and stories beat "innovative," "world-class," "game-changer."
3. **One idea per unit.** One claim per post, one thought per sentence, one job per section.
4. **Readability target grade 6–8** for most marketing copy (8–10 for B2B/technical).
5. **Voice first when it spans outputs.** Run §5, reuse the `VOICE PROFILE`, never re-derive style mid-project.
6. **Never invent** facts, credibility, testimonials, or customer evidence.
7. **Earn every CTA.** A CTA must be warranted by the content and approved by the user.
8. **Kill AI tells.** Strip "In today's rapidly evolving landscape," throat-clearing bridges, forced casualness, and engagement-bait closers.
